\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Hong Kong transport data is unusually rich and unusually siloed: KMB, MTR, the
Transport Department, and the Observatory each publish open or paid feeds, but
each lives behind a separate contract, rate limit, and access discipline.
Bilateral integration does not scale, and as policy questions about privacy,
attribution, and downstream redistribution sharpen, the gap is increasingly
visible. We design and implement a federated Hong Kong transport dataspace on
the Eclipse Dataspace Components (EDC) Minimum Viable Dataspace (MVD)~--- three
participants (\emph{HK Transport Hub} as data provider, \emph{HKTaxi} as a
commercial consumer, and \emph{HKU Transport Lab} as a dual-role academic
participant), four connectors aggregated by a Federated Catalog Node, real
upstream data fetched from \texttt{data.etabus.gov.hk} and synthetic but
realistic HKU research data, two custom ODRL policy functions (a permission
constraint and a duty) implemented as EDC \texttt{AtomicConstraintRuleFunction}s,
verifiable-credential-driven access where the participant tier is asserted by
the dataspace issuer rather than read from the holder DID, push transfer to
S3-compatible storage (MinIO deployed in-cluster), and live DCP credential
issuance that drives the issuer's
\texttt{CREATED}$\rightarrow$\texttt{REQUESTING}$\rightarrow$\texttt{REQUESTED}%
$\rightarrow$\texttt{ISSUED} state machine end-to-end. All claims are backed by
seven reproducible demonstration scripts and twenty-four captured evidence
files.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}

Hong Kong's mobility ecosystem is a textbook case of \emph{rich data, missing
plumbing}. The Transport Department publishes open data on traffic volume,
speed, and incidents; KMB exposes a public real-time arrival API at
\code{data.etabus.gov.hk}; MTR releases patronage and station-throughput
statistics; the Observatory contributes weather and air-quality streams that
modulate everything above. Each feed is technically reachable, but their
\emph{combination}~--- the operation a route-planning app, a transit-equity
study, or a pandemic-mobility dashboard actually needs~--- requires bilateral
licensing, ad-hoc credential management, custom rate-limit handling, and
manual attribution discipline. There is no shared catalog, no shared identity
layer, and no machine-enforceable usage policy beyond ``you signed a PDF''.

As traffic data grows in volume and policy questions sharpen~--- privacy of
movement, attribution of derived analytics, downstream redistribution, the
liability of consumers who re-package public-feed data into commercial
products~--- this bilateral model does not scale. A
\emph{dataspace}~\cite{idsa-ram4} is the architectural answer the
IDSA / Gaia-X / Eclipse Dataspace Components communities have converged on:
keep data sovereign at the producer, but standardise the discovery,
contracting, transfer, and enforcement layers so that joining the federation
means \emph{speaking the protocol}, not signing $N$ separate contracts.

\subsection{Project goals}

This project realises a small but complete federated dataspace for Hong Kong
transport data. Concretely it must (a) instantiate the IDSA reference flow
end-to-end with real upstream data, (b) include at least one dual-role
participant, (c) expose a Federated Catalog Node that crawls every provider
on a configurable interval, (d) enforce policies that key off
\emph{verifiable-credential claims}~--- not on participant identifiers~---
and (e) demonstrate live credential issuance over DCP. Performance
benchmarking and production hardening (TLS, KMS-backed keys,
high-availability storage) are explicit non-goals; we discuss them in §\ref{sec:future}.

\subsection{Related dataspaces and applications}
\label{sec:related}

We position our work against four reference points.

\textbf{IDSA Reference Architecture Model 4.0}~\cite{idsa-ram4} is the
canonical specification we conform to: it defines the role separation
(connectors, identity providers, clearing house, vocabulary providers), the
DSP message catalog, and the policy-language attachment via ODRL.

\textbf{Eclipse Dataspace Components (EDC)}~\cite{edc-project} is the most
actively-developed open-source implementation of the IDSA RAM in
production-grade Java; the Minimum Viable Dataspace (MVD)~\cite{edc-mvd} is
its canonical example deployment, shipping a Catalog Server, identity hubs,
and a standalone issuer service. Our work is a derivative of MVD.

\textbf{Mobility Data Space (MDS), Germany}~\cite{mds-de}, operated by DRM
Datenraum Mobilität GmbH, is the closest production analogue to our scenario:
a federated mobility dataspace whose participants negotiate over IDS-style
contracts. MDS, however, currently relies on bilateral contract templates
rather than fine-grained, machine-evaluated ODRL constraints, and does not
publicly expose its policy functions. Our project demonstrates that the
finer-grained model is feasible at the size of a course project.

\textbf{Catena-X}~\cite{catenax} is the largest currently-deployed dataspace
on the EDC stack, in the automotive supply-chain domain. It demonstrates
verifiable-credential-driven access at industrial scale; we adopt the same
trust pattern (issuer-attested membership credentials feeding into the
PolicyEngine) at small scale, on transport data instead.

To our knowledge no academic prototype combines IDSA-compliant policy
enforcement with real Hong Kong transport upstreams; existing transport-data
sharing studies either skip the policy layer or stop at API mediation.

\subsection{Contributions}

This report describes the following concrete contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A federated topology with three semantic actors~--- \emph{HK Transport
    Hub} (the provider, internally split across two connectors plus a federated
    catalog server), \emph{HKTaxi} (a commercial consumer), and \emph{HKU
    Transport Lab} (a dual-role academic participant)~--- spread across 21
    pods in a K3d cluster.
  \item A Federated Catalog Node that periodically crawls every provider
    connector and exposes a unified catalog containing 12 datasets across
    4 providers (§\ref{sec:data}).
  \item Real-world data integration: live KMB ETA JSON pulled on demand from
    the public \code{data.etabus.gov.hk} endpoint, KMB route master, plus
    realistic HK Transport Department, MTR-patronage, and HKU research-archive
    assets registered as catalog offers.
  \item \textbf{VC-claim-driven access control.} A custom ODRL constraint
    (\code{participantTier}) backed by an EDC
    \code{AtomicConstraintRuleFunction} that reads tier from a
    \emph{MembershipCredential} claim issued by the dataspace issuer, rather
    than substring-matching on the holder DID.
  \item An ODRL \textbf{Duty} (\code{AttributionDutyFunction}) that the
    consumer must satisfy on every pull, demonstrating obligation-style
    policies.
  \item Live DCP credential issuance: HKU obtains a fresh
    \code{FoobarCredential} on demand from the issuer service, exercising the
    \code{CREATED}$\rightarrow$\code{REQUESTING}$\rightarrow$\code{REQUESTED}%
    $\rightarrow$\code{ISSUED} state machine end-to-end.
  \item Push transfer to S3-compatible storage (MinIO deployed in-cluster),
    exercising the EDC \code{AmazonS3-PUSH} transfer type.
  \item Reproducible deployment: K3d + Terraform + Helm, plus a suite of
    eight end-to-end demonstration scripts that exercise every claim above
    and capture evidence to \code{final/screenshots/} for after-the-fact
    inspection.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The dataspace abstraction}

A \emph{dataspace}~\cite{idsa-ram4} is a federation of independent participants
who share data under machine-enforceable usage policies. Three properties
distinguish it from a data platform or a federated query engine:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sovereignty.} Data stays at the producer; access is brokered
    via a contract, not copied to a central store.
  \item \textbf{Decentralised identity.} Participants prove who they are via
    Decentralised Identifiers (DIDs)~\cite{w3c-did} and Verifiable
    Credentials issued by trusted authorities, not via central IAM.
  \item \textbf{Policy enforcement at the connector.} Usage policies travel
    with the data (in the contract agreement) and are checked by both sides'
    connectors before, during, and after transfer.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Eclipse Dataspace Components}

EDC~\cite{edc-project} is a modular Java framework. The pieces relevant to
this project are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Connector} (Control Plane + Data Plane)~--- speaks DSP,
    negotiates contracts, orchestrates transfers.
  \item \textbf{Catalog Server}~--- a connector specialised to act as a
    federated catalog node, periodically crawling other connectors' offers.
  \item \textbf{Identity Hub}~--- stores Verifiable Credentials for a
    participant; speaks DCP to credential issuers and to verifiers.
  \item \textbf{Issuer Service}~--- a standalone runtime that issues VCs
    using the DCP state machine.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Protocols: DSP, DCP, ODRL}

Three protocols carry all interactions in our deployment:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dataspace Protocol (DSP)}~--- HTTP-based; defines the
    \code{CatalogRequest}, \code{ContractRequest}, and \code{TransferRequest}
    message exchanges and their state machines.
  \item \textbf{Decentralised Claims Protocol (DCP)}~--- used both for
    credential \emph{issuance} (which we demonstrate live) and for credential
    \emph{presentation} during contract negotiation.
  \item \textbf{Open Digital Rights Language (ODRL)}~\cite{w3c-odrl}~--- the
    JSON-LD vocabulary used to express policies as a triple of
    \code{permission}, \code{prohibition}, and \code{duty}, each with
    optional \code{constraint}s.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Trust model: DIDs and Verifiable Credentials}
\label{sec:trust}

Every participant has a \code{did:web} identifier resolvable, in our cluster,
to a DID document hosting the participant's public key. The dataspace issuer
(\code{did:web:dataspace-issuer-service\%3A10016:issuer}) issues
\code{MembershipCredential}s naming the holder and a participant tier
(\code{COMMERCIAL}, \code{ACADEMIC}, or \code{PROVIDER}). These credentials
are presented as JWT verifiable presentations during DSP negotiation and are
the input to our policy functions (§\ref{sec:custom-ext}).

\section{Scenario and Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{The Hong Kong transport scenario}

Three semantic actors share data through the dataspace.

\textbf{HK Transport Hub} is the data provider. In a production deployment it
would be a Hong Kong governmental or industry organisation (for example, the
Transport Department or a designated data trustee) that aggregates and
re-publishes operator feeds. In our deployment the Hub is split across three
connectors: \code{provider-qna} (KMB live ETA and route master),
\code{provider-manufacturing} (HK Traffic Department incidents, MTR patronage,
academic-research archive), and \code{provider-catalog-server} (the federated
catalog node that crawls the other two and is itself a catalog publisher).

\textbf{HKTaxi} is a commercial consumer~--- a route-optimisation app that
needs the KMB feed to recommend trips. It holds a
\code{MembershipCredential} with \code{participantTier=COMMERCIAL}.

\textbf{HKU Transport Lab} is a dual-role academic participant. As a
consumer, it pulls KMB and traffic data for transit-equity research; as a
provider, it publishes a research-archive asset back into the federation
that other participants can negotiate against. Its
\code{MembershipCredential} carries \code{participantTier=ACADEMIC}.

A \emph{Dataspace Issuer Service} sits outside the producer/consumer split
and acts as the trust anchor: it issues all three actors their
\code{MembershipCredential} and, on demand, additional credential types
(§\ref{sec:eval-d5}). The relationships are summarised in
Figure~\ref{fig:scenario}.

\subsection{Data assets}

Twelve datasets are visible in the federated catalog. The full inventory is
listed in §\ref{sec:data}; design-wise, the assets are partitioned into:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Open transport data} from KMB and the HK Transport Department,
    available to consumers carrying any tier.
  \item \emph{Sensitive transport data} (real-time ETA, MTR patronage),
    gated by the \code{participantTier} permission constraint.
  \item \emph{Academic-research datasets}, tagged for academic consumption
    only; the HKU dual-role asset (\code{hku-transit-equity-2026}) belongs
    here.
  \item \emph{Attribution-bearing data}, which carries an additional ODRL
    duty (\code{asset-with-attribution}; see §\ref{sec:eval-d7}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Trust architecture}

Three credential types drive policy decisions in our scenario:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{DataProcessorCredential} (preloaded)~--- gates whether a
    holder may participate in the dataspace at all.
  \item \code{MembershipCredential} (preloaded; carries the
    \code{participantTier} claim)~--- drives the custom permission
    constraint.
  \item \code{FoobarCredential} (issued live during the demo)~--- exercises
    the DCP issuance flow.
\end{itemize}

The credentials are issued at deploy time by the Dataspace Issuer Service
into each participant's identity hub via a Helm configmap, except for
\code{FoobarCredential}, which is requested by HKU at runtime.

\subsection{Federated topology}

A single Catalog Server crawls \code{provider-qna},
\code{provider-manufacturing}, and the HKU provider-side connector at a fixed
interval (configured via \code{edc.catalog.cache.execution.period.seconds}),
indexes their offers, and exposes a unified catalog endpoint. A consumer
issues a single catalog request and receives the union~--- in our deployment,
12 offers~--- without knowing the underlying provider topology.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Deployment topology}

The dataspace runs on a local single-node K3d cluster, provisioned via
Terraform + Helm. The \code{mvd} namespace contains 21 pods, organised in
the four sub-clusters shown in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}: the provider side
(catalog server, two provider connectors, identity hub, vault, Postgres),
two consumer sides (HKTaxi and HKU, each with control plane, data plane,
identity hub, vault, Postgres), and the identity sub-cluster (issuer
service, DID server, issuer Postgres).

Cluster-internal DNS (\code{hku-identityhub},
\code{dataspace-issuer-service}, \code{provider-identityhub}, \dots)
resolves the \code{did:web} identifiers; \code{ingress-nginx} exposes
selected endpoints at \code{http://127.0.0.1/<actor>/<role>/...} so that
the demo scripts can drive negotiation, transfer, and credential
issuance from a developer laptop.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

We deliberately \emph{describe} code in this section rather than reproduce it;
the public repository (§\ref{sec:repo}) contains the full sources.

\subsection{Stock MVD baseline (Part 1 of the assignment)}

Before extending the MVD we ran the canonical 2-connector setup
(\code{provider-qna}, \code{consumer}) end-to-end: catalog discovery
$\rightarrow$ contract negotiation $\rightarrow$ HTTP-PULL transfer, plus a
separate HTTP-PUSH transfer. This validated our local toolchain (K3d, Helm,
Postman / curl, \code{jq}) and gave us reference logs to compare our
extensions against.

\subsection{Custom EDC extensions}
\label{sec:custom-ext}

Our policy contributions live under \code{extensions/dcp-impl} as \textbf{two
new \code{AtomicConstraintRuleFunction} implementations plus the registration
glue} that hooks them into EDC's \code{PolicyEngine}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{ParticipantTierFunction} is an
    \code{AtomicConstraintRuleFunction<Permission>} bound to the ODRL property
    \code{participantTier}. On each policy evaluation it receives the
    \code{ParticipantAgent} (built from the presented VP), looks up
    \emph{MembershipCredential} VCs in the agent's claims, extracts the
    \code{participantTier} field, and matches it against the right operand.
    This replaces an earlier DID-substring approach we discarded as unsafe
    (see §\ref{sec:disc} problem~1).

  \item \code{AttributionDutyFunction} is an
    \code{AtomicConstraintRuleFunction<Duty>}. Bound to a duty whose left
    operand is \code{attribution}, the function requires the consumer to
    declare an attribution string before each pull and verifies that the
    required marker is present in the duty payload. Demonstrates
    obligation-style ODRL: the policy is satisfied not by a property of the
    holder but by an action the holder commits to.

  \item \code{PolicyEvaluationExtension} (modified)~--- the existing
    \code{dcp-impl} registration extension was extended to register the two
    functions above with the appropriate policy scopes (negotiation, transfer,
    catalog).
\end{itemize}

For DCP issuance we did \emph{not} write a new attestation source: EDC ships
a \code{demo} attestation factory (\code{DemoAttestationSource} /
\code{DemoAttestationsExtension}, upstream Cofinity-X code) that we leverage
as-is. Our contribution there is a data-side fix~--- inserting a row in the
issuer's \code{membership\_attestations} Postgres table for HKU so that the
attestation pipeline does not NPE when HKU requests a credential
(§\ref{sec:disc} problem~4).

\subsection{Verifiable Credential lifecycle}

Two paths coexist in the deployment:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Preloaded VCs} (used for routine demos): each participant's
    identity hub is seeded at deploy time from a Helm configmap containing
    \code{DataProcessorCredential} and \code{MembershipCredential} JWTs
    signed by the issuer.
  \item \textbf{Live issuance} (Demo~5): HKU sends a
    \code{CredentialRequestMessage} via DCP; the issuer service evaluates an
    attestation pipeline (Postgres lookup + the \code{demo} source), signs
    a fresh \code{FoobarCredential} JWT, and pushes it back to HKU's
    identity hub.
\end{itemize}

The preloaded \code{MembershipCredential} JWTs were re-signed with the
issuer's Ed25519 private key to inject the \code{participantTier} claim;
the resulting tokens are checked into
\code{deployment/assets/credentials/k8s/<role>/membership-credential.json}.

\subsection{Federated catalog crawler}

The Catalog Server is configured (via
\code{edc.catalog.cache.execution.period.seconds}) to crawl each provider
connector at a fixed interval. Targeted DSP endpoints are listed in a
configmap (one entry per provider); the server materialises a unified
catalog and exposes it via the same DSP catalog endpoint that any
single-provider connector exposes.

\subsection{Push transfer to S3 (MinIO)}

We deployed a MinIO stateful service in-cluster with a single bucket
(\code{edc-push-bucket}) and added the \code{data-plane-aws-s3} EDC
extension (version 0.7.0; see §\ref{sec:disc} problem~5) to the dataplane
build. Demo~2's transfer uses
\code{transferType: "AmazonS3-PUSH"} and a \code{dataDestination} carrying
\code{endpointOverride}, \code{accessKeyId}, and \code{secretAccessKey}.
The provider's dataplane fetches the upstream KMB JSON and writes it to
the bucket.

\subsection{ODRL policy expressions}

Two non-trivial policies were authored:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A \emph{Permission} with an ODRL \code{Constraint} bound to
    \code{participantTier == COMMERCIAL} (or \code{ACADEMIC}), evaluated by
    \code{ParticipantTierFunction}.
  \item A \emph{Duty} requiring an \code{attribution} declaration,
    evaluated by \code{AttributionDutyFunction} on every contract use.
\end{itemize}

Both are attached to assets at offer-creation time via the EDC
management API.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Sources}

The dataspace mixes three categories of data: live external feeds, snapshot
files of public Hong Kong transport data, and synthetic but realistic
research archives.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Live KMB ETA / routes}: pulled at request time from the
    public KMB open-data endpoint at \code{data.etabus.gov.hk}~\cite{kmb-od}.
    Latencies are typically below 200~ms; a single ETA request returns
    roughly $350$~KB of JSON, a single route request roughly $250$~KB.
  \item \textbf{HK Transport Department incidents} and \textbf{MTR
    patronage}: stored as static JSON files under the provider connector's
    asset registry. The schemas mirror the published HKTD and MTR open-data
    formats; the snapshots are dated to allow reproducibility.
  \item \textbf{HKU research archive}: a synthetic but realistic CSV
    representing a transit-equity study, registered as the HKU dual-role
    asset (\code{hku-transit-equity-2026}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We exercise the system through seven scripted demonstrations, all under
\code{final/scripts/}. Each captures evidence to \code{final/screenshots/}
so results are reproducible after the fact. The scripts are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:scripts}.

\subsection{Demo~1 --- Negotiation and HTTP-PULL transfer}

\code{demo-l2-transfer.sh}. HKTaxi's connector fetches the catalog from
the Catalog Server, selects the KMB live-ETA offer, negotiates a
contract, requests a transfer in pull mode, exchanges an EDR, and
finally pulls live KMB JSON from the provider's data plane. The full
seven-step flow is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}.

\noindent\textbf{Evidence:} \code{01-catalog.json},
\code{02-negotiation-finalized.json}, \code{03-transfer-started.json},
\code{04-edr.json}, \code{05-real-kmb-data.json}.

\subsection{Demo~2 --- Push transfer to S3 (MinIO)}

\code{demo-push-s3.sh}. The same negotiation as Demo~1 but with
\code{transferType: AmazonS3-PUSH}. The provider's dataplane writes a
$352$\,KB KMB JSON object into the in-cluster MinIO bucket; the script
verifies the object via \code{mc ls} inside the MinIO pod.

\noindent\textbf{Evidence:} \code{22-s3-push-bucket-listing.json},
\code{23-s3-push-evidence.txt}.

\subsection{Demo~3 --- HKU dual role}

\code{demo-hku-dual-role.sh} + \code{demo-hku-as-consumer.sh}. HKU first
publishes its research asset and exposes it via DSP; HKTaxi then
negotiates with HKU, gets an EDR, and pulls the HKU data; finally HKU
itself acts as a consumer and pulls KMB data from \code{provider-qna}.
This proves both directions of the dual-role pattern in a single run.

\noindent\textbf{Evidence:} \code{13a-hku-as-provider-catalog.json}
through \code{13d-hku-research-data.json},
\code{16-hku-consumer-catalog.json}, \code{17-hku-academic-agreement.json},
\code{18-hku-academic-data-pulled.json}.

\subsection{Demo~4 --- Federated catalog crawl}

\code{federated-crawler.sh}. The Catalog Server's unified catalog is
queried once; the response lists offers from \code{qna},
\code{manufacturing}, \code{catalog-server}, and \code{hku}, demonstrating
that the crawler ran on its configured cadence and merged the four
underlying catalogs (Table~\ref{tab:assets}).

\noindent\textbf{Evidence:} \code{14-federated-crawler.txt}.

\subsection{Demo~5 --- Live DCP credential issuance}
\label{sec:eval-d5}

\code{demo-live-issuance.sh}. HKU's identity hub initiates a
\code{CredentialRequestMessage} to the issuer service. The DCP state
machine progresses
\code{CREATED}$\rightarrow$\code{REQUESTING}$\rightarrow$\code{REQUESTED}%
$\rightarrow$\code{ISSUED}, verified by tailing the identity hub log
filtered on the request ID. The issuer evaluates an attestation
pipeline (Postgres membership lookup + the \code{demo} attestation
factory) and signs a fresh \code{FoobarCredential} JWT, which is then
pushed into HKU's wallet. The script decodes the resulting JWT and
confirms \code{iss} = issuer DID, \code{sub} = HKU DID, \code{iat} =
the demo's timestamp (i.e.\ not the configmap-baked \code{2023-12-12}
of preloaded VCs), and \code{vc.type} including
\code{FoobarCredential}.

\noindent\textbf{Evidence:} \code{24-live-credential-issuance.txt}.

\subsection{Demo~6 --- Custom Permission constraint (\code{participantTier})}

\code{demo-custom-constraint.sh}. Two flows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item HKTaxi (\code{participantTier=COMMERCIAL}) succeeds in pulling a
    \code{commercial-only} asset.
  \item HKU (\code{participantTier=ACADEMIC}) is rejected with an explicit
    policy-violation message when requesting the same asset.
\end{itemize}

This proves the rule reads the VC claim, not the holder DID: if it were
reading the DID, both holders would be indistinguishable to the rule.

\noindent\textbf{Evidence:} \code{08-policy-rejection.json},
\code{15-custom-constraint-rejection.json},
\code{17-hku-academic-agreement.json} (positive case for the ACADEMIC
asset).

\subsection{Demo~7 --- Attribution Duty}
\label{sec:eval-d7}

\code{demo-attribution-duty.sh}. The asset's policy carries a duty:
``on use, declare an attribution string''. When the consumer satisfies
the duty (the attribution payload is present and well-formed), the
pull succeeds; when it does not, the pull is rejected by
\code{AttributionDutyFunction} before reaching the data plane.

\noindent\textbf{Evidence:} \code{19-attribution-duty-offer.json},
\code{20-attribution-success.json}, \code{21-attribution-rejection.json}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Problems overcome}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Tier-from-DID was not real policy enforcement.}
    Our first version of the tier check matched on substrings of the
    holder DID. This worked, but was a lie: the provider was trusting
    the \emph{name} of the participant, not a credential issued about
    it. We replaced it with a VC-claim lookup
    (§\ref{sec:custom-ext}, \code{ParticipantTierFunction}), which
    required us to inject the tier claim into the preloaded
    \code{MembershipCredentials} and re-sign each one with the
    issuer's Ed25519 private key.

  \item \textbf{DCP credential request body schema.} First attempts
    using \code{credentialType} as the credential descriptor returned
    HTTP~400. Reading the EDC end-to-end test
    (\code{tests/end2end/.../CredentialIssuanceEndToEndTest.java})
    revealed that the correct shape uses \code{type} plus \code{id}
    referencing a credential definition.

  \item \textbf{Issuer participant context stuck in \code{CREATED}.}
    The seeded issuer participant context started inactive; credential
    requests failed with HTTP~500 until activated via
    \code{POST /participants/\{ctx\}/state?isActive=true}. This is
    not obvious from the management-API documentation but is required
    once per fresh deployment.

  \item \textbf{\code{AttestationPipelineImpl.evaluate} NPE for HKU.}
    The default seed only registered the consumer in
    \code{membership\_attestations}; the attestation source returned
    \code{null} for HKU, NPEing inside the pipeline. We inserted an
    HKU row with \code{membership\_type=3} as part of the
    cluster bootstrap.

  \item \textbf{EDC AWS S3 module versioning.}
    \code{data-plane-aws-s3:0.14.1} does not exist on Maven Central:
    EDC's Technology-AWS modules are versioned independently of
    EDC core. We pinned to \code{0.7.0} and added a separate
    \code{edc-aws} version line in \code{libs.versions.toml}. The
    failure mode was a Gradle dependency-resolution error during
    dataplane build, rather than a runtime bug.

  \item \textbf{\code{participant\_context\_id} is a legacy DID.} The
    issuer's Postgres still keys some tables on
    \code{did:web:localhost\%3A10100} from a pre-K8s seed, not on the
    current \code{did:web:dataspace-issuer-service\%3A10016:issuer}.
    Demos that touch the issuer admin API have to base64 the legacy
    DID for those particular path parameters.

  \item \textbf{Custom ODRL constraints cannot be pure JSON.}
    Out of the box, EDC ships only a couple of built-in constraint
    functions; a property like \code{participantTier} requires a Java
    extension that registers the function with
    \code{PolicyEngine.registerFunction(\dots)}. This is documented in
    EDC, but worth flagging as a real limitation for adopters who
    hoped policy was config-only.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item All endpoints are plain HTTP; no TLS termination, no mTLS, no
    certificate-based DID verification.
  \item Single-region, single-node K3d. No HA, no Postgres backups.
  \item Keys live in plain configmaps and Vault dev mode (in-memory,
    cleared on pod restart).
  \item Only one ``real'' upstream is wired (KMB); MTR / HKO weather
    were planned but stayed at the design stage.
  \item The Duty (§\ref{sec:eval-d7} attribution) is checked
    synchronously by the consumer connector; production deployments
    would want an audit log on a separate trust domain so that the
    consumer cannot trivially fabricate satisfaction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Lessons learned}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{EDC's E2E tests are the most reliable spec.} When
    documentation and code disagreed, the tests under
    \code{tests/end2end/...} were always right. This was particularly
    true for DCP message bodies (problem~2 above).
  \item \textbf{Credential plumbing is the time sink.} The policy
    \emph{logic} took hours; getting the right VC into the right hub
    at the right time, and re-signed when claims changed, took days.
    Tooling for VC re-signing and verification would meaningfully
    reduce the cost of running a small dataspace.
  \item \textbf{\code{did:web} works locally if your DNS does.}
    Cluster-internal DNS plus consistent
    \code{did:web:<service>\%3A<port>:<id>} naming let us drop
    \code{did:key} entirely and made every DID resolvable from any
    other pod in the cluster.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\subsection{Conclusion}

We have built and demonstrated a federated Hong Kong transport
dataspace that exercises every capability the assignment lists for
Part~2~--- three semantic participants (one in a dual role), real
data, a federated catalog node that crawls four providers, and
advanced policy enforcement~--- plus elements of the optional Part~3
in the form of custom ODRL constraints and duties expressed as Java
extensions. All claims are backed by reproducible scripts and
captured evidence under \code{final/screenshots/}.

\subsection{Future work}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Production hardening.} TLS / mTLS everywhere,
    KMS-backed keys, Postgres backups, Helm chart values for prod.
  \item \textbf{More providers.} Wire HKO weather, real MTR (or a
    synthetic MTR feed if no API is publicly available), and a
    citizen-reporting feed; this would also stress the federated
    catalog crawler at a more realistic provider count.
  \item \textbf{Marketplace layer.} Pricing, settlement, and a UI on
    top of the catalog~--- the optional Part~3 of the assignment.
  \item \textbf{Consent revocation.} Surface a revocation flow for
    VCs and verify that connectors honour the revocation when a
    previously-valid VC is invalidated mid-transfer.
  \item \textbf{Observability.} Prometheus + Grafana dashboards on
    the connector and dataplane metrics that EDC already exposes.
  \item \textbf{Richer credential vocabularies.} KYC,
    operating-licence, region-of-incorporation; let policies stack
    constraints on multiple VCs and expose tools to compose them.
\end{itemize}
